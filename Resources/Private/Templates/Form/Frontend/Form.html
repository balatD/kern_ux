The error summary comes first, before the form element, so that a keyboard or screen reader user meets it immediately after submitting rather than having to hunt through the fields. The progress indicator only appears for multi-page forms - on a single-page form "Schritt 1 von 1" is noise. section points at the error summary, not at the form. ext:form puts the value in the form action's fragment, so it decides where the browser lands after a submit - and with id="{form.identifier}" on the form element, which sits *after* the summary, it used to scroll the visitor straight past the one thing they needed to read. Aiming it at the summary also lands the focus there, because the summary carries tabindex="-1": fragment navigation focuses its target when the target is focusable. That is the whole reason no form JavaScript is needed here - the same reasoning as the Kopfzeile, where a mandated behaviour should not depend on scripting. On a successful submit the fragment simply matches nothing and the next page opens at the top, which is where a fresh step should start anyway.
Tag syntax rather than inline: an inline f:translate carrying an arguments array is output verbatim by Fluid instead of evaluated.
